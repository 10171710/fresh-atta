Soft phulka rotis: the 70% hydration method

LH
Layla Haddad
Recipe Developer & Baker

The biggest misconception about making soft rotis is that you need oil, ghee, or milk in the dough. You do not. All you need is fresh stone-ground flour, the correct water-to-flour ratio by weight, and a patient rest period for gluten hydration.

The golden formula: 70% hydration

Hydration simply means the weight of water relative to the weight of flour. Stone-ground whole wheat atta contains the intact outer bran and germ, both of which are thirstier than commercial bleached flour. If you only use 55% water, the bran steals moisture from the starch, leaving the cooked roti dry and brittle.

For every 100 grams of Millstone Sharbati Atta, use exactly 70 grams (or ml) of warm water. For an average family batch of 8 rotis (250 g flour), use 175 ml of warm water.

The 4 essential steps

  1. The Initial Mix (2 minutes): Add 80% of the water to the flour in a wide parat. Mix with your fingers until a rough, shaggy mass forms. Sprinkle the remaining 20% over dry pockets. Do not knead aggressively yet!
  2. The 15-Minute Autolyse: Cover the rough dough with a damp muslin cloth and walk away for 15 minutes. During this rest, the bran softens and gluten bonds form naturally without mechanical sweat.
  3. The Quick Knead (3 minutes): Return to the bowl. Punch down with your knuckles for just 2 to 3 minutes. You will feel the dough transform into a silky, supple dough that springs back when pressed.
  4. Rolling without excess dry flour: Roll lightly with wrist pressure from center outward. Dust only once with dry flour; excessive dry flour scorches on the tawa and creates bitter spots.
Steam is the only leavening agent in a phulka. If your tawa is hot enough, trapped steam inflates the two layers into a sphere within three seconds.

Tawa timing and flame transition

Preheat a heavy cast iron tawa until a drop of water sizzles and dances. Place your rolled roti gently:

  • Side 1 (15-20 seconds): Wait until tiny pale blisters appear on the surface. Flip immediately.
  • Side 2 (30-40 seconds): Cook until golden-brown freckles develop underneath. Press the edges gently with a clean cloth.
  • The Flame (3-5 seconds): Transfer directly onto a medium open burner flame with tongs. Watch it balloon completely, then transfer to an insulated roti dabba lined with a cotton towel.

Why they stay soft until lunchtime

Because the 70% hydration gelled the starch completely during cooking, moisture is locked inside the molecular matrix. Even four hours later in an office tiffin, your phulkas will tear like silk.
