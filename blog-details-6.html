How we keep flour below 40 degrees C

In commercial grain milling, speed is money. High-speed industrial roller mills and high-RPM electric chakkis spin at 800 to 1,400 revolutions per minute, ejecting flour at temperatures between 60 °C and 75 °C. At that heat, the delicate live oils in the wheat germ are practically cooked before they ever touch your kitchen pan.

Meera Kapoor Head Miller 2 Sep 2026 6 min read

The chemistry of milling heat

Wheat germ is packed with natural tocopherols (Vitamin E), polyunsaturated fatty acids, and active enzymes. When flour temperatures exceed 42 °C, three things begin to happen:

  • Thermal oxidation of germ oils: The rich, sweet hazelnut fragrance degrades into a flat, chalky aroma.
  • Premature starch gelatinization: Damaged starch loses its capacity to absorb water steadily during kneading.
  • Enzyme denaturation: Amylase enzymes that naturally break down starch into sweet malt sugars during dough resting are destroyed.
If flour feels uncomfortably hot to your bare palm as it leaves the chute, the mill has already killed its soul.

Our three cold-milling safeguards

At Millstone & Co., we maintain a strict ceiling: flour never leaves the stone spout above 38.5 °C. To guarantee this across both summer and winter, we rely on three engineering practices:

1. Ultra-Low RPM

Our 750 kg granite stones turn at an unhurried 280 RPM. Lower velocity reduces friction heat by over 65% compared to commercial chakkis.

2. Measured Feed Rate

Vibratory hoppers feed whole grains at a steady 45 kg per hour, preventing grain bunching and friction bottlenecks between stones.

3. Passive Aeration Trays

Flour flows over food-grade stainless cooling flumes where ambient filtered air brings temperature down to 24 °C before bagging.

Live digital monitoring on every run

Every mill unit at Granary Lane is fitted with twin infrared thermal sensors positioned at the stone throat and the discharge chute. If flour temperature approaches 40 °C, the feed controller automatically throttles the grain flow and increases airflow.

Taste the cold-milled difference

When you take your first bite of a roti made from cold-milled Sharbati atta, the difference is immediate: clean wheat sweetness, zero bitterness, and a supple chew that keeps fresh all day.
